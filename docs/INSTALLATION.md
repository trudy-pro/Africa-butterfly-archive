# African Butterfly Archive — Project concept and touchscreen installation

## Objective

Natural-history archives are often understood as static collections. **African Butterfly Archive** explored how digital and spatial media could reinterpret scientific and archival knowledge as a dynamic, approachable experience.

The project combined accessible visual representations of African butterflies' **wing patterns, colours, habitats and migrations** with simple scientific observations. It asked how a digital archive could change the ways people perceive, navigate and collectively construct natural history.

An important motivation was making natural-history information visually engaging and accessible to non-scientists, particularly in an African context.

## Two connected forms

### Touchscreen installation

The archive was presented through a **touchscreen display installation**. Visitors approached the screen and explored butterfly imagery through direct touch, selecting species through visual features such as colours, habitats and wings.

The display operated as both an image-based exhibition surface and an interactive archive. Its playful interface invited visitors to explore in their own way rather than follow a fixed sequence.

### Website

A companion web experience expanded access to the archive outside the installation setting and provided another medium for the project to unfold.

[Website](https://africa-butterfly-archive.netlify.app/)

The source corresponding to the final live website has not yet been added to this repository.

## Participation and living archives

The project's concept included inviting visitors to contribute their own butterfly discoveries, with submitted butterflies classified and added to the archive.

A historical source version includes `contribute.html`, described as a form for butterfly notes. That is evidence of a contribution interface, **not** verification of a completed submission-to-classification-to-publication system. The collective archive is therefore presented as a design ambition rather than a confirmed fully operational workflow.

## Research themes

- **Nature and technology:** how interactive media represent and reinterpret natural history
- **Memory and archives:** moving beyond a static specimen catalogue toward a responsive experience
- **Embodied interaction:** touch as a way to browse, examine and discover
- **Visual accessibility:** enabling exploration by non-scientists through colour, patterns, wing characteristics and habitat
- **Collective knowledge:** how audiences could participate in shaping an open, evolving archive
